## Step 3: Proposed arc and motif map

This design uses **Night Shift** as an open-ended setting: a system comes online, settles into a working rhythm, meets rising pressure, then shuts down with a distinct final signal. It doesn’t depend on the sample auditions; sample assignments can change while these musical roles stay intact.

### Motifs

| Motif | Character | Musical shape |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Shift Motor** | Persistent forward motion | A low-register phrase with a held opening note, a displaced pickup, and a shortened return. It should feel like a mechanism settling into a cycle, rather than a kick doubling the bass. |
| **B — Relay Code** | Metallic response | A compact upper-register phrase that rises by a minor third, repeats its top note, then cuts off. Use it to answer gaps in A. |
| **C — Pressure Fall** | Growing tension | A descending four-note line, starting spacious and becoming more compressed. Let it cross A’s phrase boundaries as the pressure builds. |
| **D — Shift Signal** | Identity and closure | A short rising call that turns downward at the end. Introduce it in fragments early; bring it back in a longer, more conclusive form for the coda. |

### Structural arc

The opening gives D and a few isolated metallic accents room to register. A then establishes the main motion, with B answering it. Repetitions gradually add layers and alter the phrase endings; C enters to increase tension while A keeps the track grounded.

At the anomaly, shift accents and let C cut across the established phrases, but keep some part of A moving so the track doesn’t reset. The return tightens the groove and brings D forward. The final section reaches full weight, then the coda strips the motion down into D’s closing flourish and a firm final hit. **No fade-out.**
